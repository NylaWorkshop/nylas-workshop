In 1348, when the Black Death was devouring Europe, Mortis was nothing more than a doctor of death. He walked the half-empty streets of a Galician city in his black hood, his beaked mask, and his bitter herbs. People feared him. He no longer expected to save anyone.

Until he found her.

Elowen lay among the bodies in an alley beside the cathedral, pale, feverish, her lips cracked. She was not human. The pointed ears, the skin still too perfect even in agony, the green gleam beneath her eyelids. An elf who had waited too long to leave the mortal world.

Mortis knelt. No one else would have.

He gave her what little he had: vinegar, garlic, a prayer he no longer believed. He stayed with her for three nights while the city burned around them. When she opened her eyes, the first thing she saw was that crow mask leaning over her face.

“You’re still breathing,” he said, his voice rough behind the leather.

She smiled, weakly.

“So are you.”

<!-- page -->

That same night, when the fever broke, Elowen took his gloved hand. The ancient power she still had left — the last thread of her lineage — slipped into him like a vow.

“I don’t owe you my life,” she whispered. “I owe you something worse. That it never ends.”

Immortality was not a clean gift. It was a bond. From that moment on, whenever one of them bled, the other felt it. Every century that passed, he kept wearing the mask and she kept reading books that time could not rot.

Wars came and went, bonfires, revolutions. He learned to hide behind the figure of the plague doctor. She became the librarian of places that appear on no map. They always found the same room: tall shelves, candles, the smell of parchment and burnt herbs.

Now, on any night of this century, Elowen holds an open grimoire against her chest. She wears the same black lace dress, the same green strand falling over her eye. Mortis stands beside her, the mask tilted, a gloved finger brushing her cheek as if he still fears she might slip away.

“You’ve been reading the same passage for seven hundred years,” he murmurs.

She laughs, low, and closes the book with a soft tap.

“Because every time I read it, you’re still here.”

The little green specters float between them, remnants of that night in 1348. Mortis does not remove the mask. He no longer needs to. Elowen knows every line of the face beneath the leather, just as he knows every page she has underlined across the centuries.

Outside, the world keeps falling ill and healing. Inside the library, time stopped the day a plague doctor decided not to let an elf die.

And she, in gratitude, decided that he would not be allowed to die either.
